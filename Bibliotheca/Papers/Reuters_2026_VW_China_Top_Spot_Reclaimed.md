# Volkswagen, through its FAW and SAIC joint ventures, retook the top spot in China car sales in January-February 2026, as BYD fell to fourth when EV purchase-tax exemptions expired

Reuters reports that in the first two months of 2026, VW's China joint ventures held 13.9% of the
passenger-vehicle market, ahead of Geely (13.8%) and Toyota's joint ventures (7.8%), while BYD fell
to fourth place at 7.1% share, its steepest sales drop since the pandemic. The article attributes
this to the expiry of purchase-tax exemptions on EVs and Beijing scaling back EV trade-in subsidies
— which hit a subsidy-dependent, budget-EV maker like BYD harder than a legacy brand with a broader
mix. It also notes the market itself contracted faster than VW's own sales, so this is described as
VW being "the largest participant in a shrinking market" rather than a demand recovery.

> **This contradicts the simple version of the thesis, and it has to stay visible rather than be
> smoothed over.** If VW's China position can reverse in two months because a subsidy expired, then
> a chunk of the "decline" documented in Mossalgue (2024) was a policy-driven EV-adoption cycle, not
> only durable margin erosion — and a policy reversal is exactly the kind of event the kill switch
> in `OBJECTIVE.md` describes: *"when a recovery in China... re-rates VW."* This source is why that
> line in the kill switch is not hypothetical. It does not by itself falsify the claim — a
> two-month, subsidy-driven reshuffle inside a shrinking market is a different thing from a
> multi-year margin recovery — but it is the first candidate event to check against, and it should
> be checked before, not after, any month where the short leg underperforms.
